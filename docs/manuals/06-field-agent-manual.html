1Welcome

A new shop signs itself up, but it cannot trade as a verified TillKnowIT shop until a person has seen it with their own eyes. That person is you.

The Field agent app is small on purpose. It has one menu entry, Shops to set up, and it does three things: it shows you the shops that are waiting, it lets you tick off the seven setup steps as you do them, and it lets you verify a shop once every step is done.

Verification matters. A verified shop is trusted by suppliers and, where a school uses the wallet, a tuck shop must be verified before its school can be. When you verify a shop you are putting your name to it: your person id is sent with every tick and with the verification.

Only people who have the field agent permission can use this app. If the server says you are not allowed, ask the platform team to give your cell number the field agent role.

2Signing in

No password. You type your cell number and the app sends you a code by SMS.

  1. Open the Field agent app and find the Sign in box.
  2. Type your number under Your cell phone number and tap Send me a code.
  3. Type the code under The code we sent you and tap Sign in.

Your sign-in lasts 12 hours. After that the app says Your sign-in has run out. Please sign in again. Use Sign out when you finish, especially on a shared phone.

Demo mode

In a demo or test version, a Development only box under the code field shows the code on the screen, so you can practise without a phone. The demo field agent is Pieter Botha, cell number 082 555 0004. In the real service the code comes only by SMS.

SMS goes through a provider called BulkSMS that has not yet been run against the live service. Until it is switched on, real codes may not reach phones.

3The list of shops waiting

When you sign in, the first screen is the card Shops waiting for you.

The help line says: New shops stay pending until every step is done and you verify them. The ones furthest along come first. So the shop at the top of the list is the one closest to being finished.

The columns of the list
ColumnWhat it shows
ShopThe name the owner gave when signing up.
MunicipalityWhere the owner said the shop is. It may be empty.
Steps doneFor example 3 of 7.
ActionsThe Open button for that shop.

If there is nothing to do, the screen says No shops are waiting for setup. If the list cannot load you see a red message with Try again.

Tap Open next to a shop to see its checklist. After you verify a shop you come back to this list with a green message such as (Shop) is verified.

4The seven setup steps

Every shop has the same seven steps. Together they prove that the shop is real, that it can sell, and that its staff know how.

The seven setup steps, as they appear on screen
StepWhat it means in practice
Device checkThe phone, tablet or till the shop will use turns on, connects and opens TillKnowIT.
Shop detailsThe shop name, address or municipality, language and contact are correct and match the real shop.
Document photosThe owner's papers (for example ID and proof the business exists) have been photographed and uploaded.
Catalogue importThe shop's products are loaded so the till can sell them.
Staff PINsEach person who will use the till has a staff record and a PIN of their own.
Test saleA sale was made on the till, from start to finish, to prove it works.
Training sign-offThe owner and staff have been shown how to sell, take cash and close the day, and agree they can do it.

The app only lists the step names. The right-hand column above is the meaning we expect behind each tick. The system does not inspect your work: a step is "done" when you say it is. See What this app does not do.

5Ticking a step, with a note

You can tick steps in any order, at the shop or afterwards. The screen is titled Setup steps for (shop name).

You see a table with four columns: Step, State, Note and Actions. The state is either Done or Still to do. A step that is done has no button; a step still to do has Mark done.

  1. Tap Open on the shop. The checklist appears.
  2. Do the work for one step with the owner. See the visit checklist.
  3. If useful, type a short note in Note for the next step you mark (optional). The hint gives examples: signed by the owner, or 12 products loaded.
  4. Tap Mark done on that step's row. The screen says (Step) is marked done. The state changes to Done and your note appears in the Note column.
  5. Repeat for the next step. To go back to the list at any time, tap Back to the list.

The note box belongs to the next step you mark, then it is emptied. Type the note for a step before you tap its Mark done. Do not tap Mark done and then type the note: it would go on the next step you mark, not on this one.

Do not tick a step that is not really finished. The system trusts you. A false tick lets a shop be verified without proper setup.

This app has no button to un-tick a step. If you tick the wrong one by mistake, tell the platform team straight away.

6Verifying a shop

Verifying is the last thing you do. It is the moment the shop stops waiting and becomes a verified shop.

Below the table, as long as any step is still to do, you see: Every step must be done before you can verify the shop. The Verify this shop button is grey.

  1. Make sure all seven rows show Done.
  2. Tap Verify this shop.
  3. You are returned to the list with (Shop) is verified. The shop disappears from the waiting list.

If a shop has already been verified, for example by another agent, you see This shop is already verified. and no button.

Verify only a shop you have seen yourself. There is no button in this app to take the verification back. The server checks that you have the right permission, and your person id is sent with the request.

After verification the owner no longer sees "waiting for verification" in the back office, and can go on with selling.

7The visit checklist

A good visit takes about two hours. Here is what to bring and what to say, so that you finish the seven steps on the day.

What to bring

  • Your phone, charged, with the Field agent app signed in. Bring a power bank.
  • Mobile data. The app needs a connection; if the shop has no network, use your own.
  • A laptop or tablet to use the back office for document photos and the catalogue (see the next chapter).
  • The device the shop will use as a till, if it is not already there, with its setup code from the platform team.
  • Your agent ID and a printed list of the seven steps.
  • A few products with barcodes to use for the test sale.
  • A sheet of paper and a pen for the owner's questions.

Before you go

  • Phone the owner and fix a time when they and their staff will be at the shop.
  • Ask the owner to have their ID, any registration papers and the staff's cell numbers ready.
  • Open Shops to set up and check how far along the shop already is.

What to say to the owner

Speak simply and kindly. Many owners worry that a computer will be hard or that someone is checking up on them. Something like this works:

  • "I am here to get your till ready, step by step. We will finish today, and then you can start selling."
  • "I need to see your ID and your papers, and take photos of them. They are kept safely and shown only to the people who must check them."
  • "Each person who sells will get their own PIN. Please do not share it. It shows who made each sale."
  • "We will make a test sale together first, so you can see that it works before real customers are waiting."
  • "When you are comfortable, I will mark your training as done. Tell me if anything is unclear."

A suggested order for the visit

  1. Device check. Switch on the device, connect it and open TillKnowIT. Tick Device check.
  2. Shop details. Check name, municipality, language and contact with the owner. Tick Shop details.
  3. Document photos. Photograph the papers in the back office, then tick Document photos.
  4. Catalogue import. Load the products in the back office, then tick Catalogue import. Note how many, for example 12 products loaded.
  5. Staff PINs. Add each person who sells and set their PINs with them. Tick Staff PINs.
  6. Test sale. Make one cash sale on the till. Tick Test sale.
  7. Training sign-off. Show the owner how to sell, give change and close the day. Ask them to try. Note signed by the owner. Tick Training sign-off, then tap Verify this shop.

If the shop is not ready (for example the owner's papers are missing), do not rush. Tick what is truly done, and leave the rest. The list shows the shop as, say, 4 of 7, so you or another agent can come back.

Protect the owner's papers. Do not photograph their ID with your own personal phone camera app and keep it. Use the back office upload only, and never send ID photos by WhatsApp.

8What this app does not do

We would rather you knew now than found out at the shop.

  • Taking document photos is not done in this app. You upload the owner's documents in the back office. Afterwards you tick Document photos here yourself.
  • Loading the catalogue is not done in this app. Products are loaded in the back office (or by the shop's own import). Afterwards you tick Catalogue import.
  • The system does not check the ticks. A step is marked, not proven. It will accept "Done" even if the photos were never uploaded. That is why your honesty matters.
  • There is no un-tick and no un-verify. Tell the platform team if you made a mistake.
  • No map, no photos, no camera. The app has no GPS or camera features today.
  • English only. The app is not yet translated.
  • SMS. The code sent by SMS has not yet run against the live provider.

Document upload in the back office accepts JPEG, PNG and PDF files up to 10 MB each, and keeps them encrypted.

9Words used in this manual

Verified shop
A shop that a field agent has visited and checked, with all seven setup steps done.
Pending
A new shop that has not yet been verified.
Back office
The web app where the owner (and you on a visit) manages products, staff, tills and documents.
Catalogue
The list of products a shop sells, with barcodes and prices.
Staff PIN
A short secret number each salesperson types on the till.
Test sale
A practice sale used to prove the till works.
Municipality
The local area (such as a metro) where the shop is.
Note
A short line you add when you mark a step, such as how many products were loaded.
